01

The objective

The model minimises one weighted sum of 14 active terms over nine heads: price change, P(up) and variance at 10, 15 and 20 minutes. Each weight is recalibrated before training so that every term's value is about the same size. The term that matters most, though, is the one whose gradient is largest.

$$L=\sum_h\lambda^{pt}_h\,\ell^{\log\cosh}_h+\lambda_{ext}\sum_h\ell^{trend}_h+\lambda_{dir}\sum_h\ell^{BCE}_h+\lambda_{var}\sum_h\ell^{NLL}_h+\lambda_{crps}\sum_h\ell^{CRPS}_h+\lambda_{ece}\sum_h\ell^{sECE}_h+0.1\lambda_{vol}\ell^{vol}+\lambda_{coh}\ell^{coh}+\underbrace{\lambda_{tp}\ell^{T\perp}+\lambda_{cas}\ell^{cas}+\lambda_{hd}\ell^{HD}+\lambda_{ife}\ell^{IFE}+\lambda_{vo}\ell^{vo}}_{\text{physics, }\lambda=0.1}$$
Share of the loss value against share of the gradient, leader model (fold −2, seed 0, served weights). Value shares come from its training log; gradient norms from a CPU probe of the served model on three batches (batch A recorded only the six largest terms). Norms do not add up, because gradients are vectors; each bar is the term's norm divided by the total norm.
How the weights were set. The pre-training pass puts each term's median value on the mean: λ ← clip(λ₀·(L̄/median)d, 0.1, 20). The small-valued soft ECE is multiplied up to 2.91, and that makes its large gradient larger.
Cosine between each term's gradient and the total gradient (probe batches B and C). Soft ECE's 0.98–0.99 means the update points almost exactly where the soft-ECE gradient points. BCE's cosine is high too, but its norm is 15–30× smaller. HD and CRPS point against the update.
02

Anatomy of each term

For each term: its formula as implemented, its gradient with respect to the head it trains, the bound on that gradient, and whether it is a proper scoring rule, a loss whose minimum is the true conditional distribution. Pick a term. The chart plots its gradient against its input, with the bound drawn in red.

Why soft ECE dominates. A simulation. Take a perfectly calibrated model and draw batches. The batch's observed up-rate still differs from the prediction by sampling noise. BCE's pull on the direction bias is proportional to that gap, so it is small and centred on zero. Soft ECE takes the absolute value of the gap, so its pull on the bias has full size, ±c, at every batch, however small the gap. Move the batch size.
03

The stability argument

What the code guarantees, in order of strength. Green steps are proven under the implemented clamps. Amber steps hold only through a floor or a clip that is far from the operating point. Red steps are not guaranteed. The measurements follow.

Adam's step bound. For any finite gradient sequence, a single weight moves at most B(t)·lr per step, with B(t) = (1−β₁)/(1−β₁ᵗ) · √((1−rᵗ)/(1−r)) · √((1−β₂ᵗ)/(1−β₂)) and r = β₁²/β₂. B(1) = 1; the supremum at 0.9 / 0.999 is 7.27. This holds with or without clipping.
Six 360-day trainings. Mean pre-clip gradient norm per epoch (the clip is 20), and the learning rate halving under the plateau schedule. Zero non-finite steps in every epoch of every run. The leader (bold) runs hottest: epochs 1–3 sit at the clip.
960 screen trials (6-hour training blocks, batch 64, 8 epochs). Mean gradient norm against the share of steps clipped. All 960 stayed finite; the 423 failures are all "more than half the steps clipped". Pick a block, and colour by a knob to see what drives the clipping.
Pass rate by clip norm (block A). The median gradient norm is about 20 in all three settings; only the threshold moved.

04

Can the loss be solved?

A proper score's minimum is set by the information in the inputs. For direction that information is tiny: at the AUC the data allow, the best possible cross-entropy is a hair below ln 2. For volatility the information is real. Move the AUC and watch how little the optimum moves.

Best achievable direction loss against AUC (a binormal model: the score is Gaussian in each class with the same variance; the Bayes posterior is σ(d·s) and AUC = Φ(d/√2)). The markers are measured AUCs.
At this AUC
Validation direction BCE per epoch of the six models (h0), against ln 2 (dashed). Most epochs sit at or above ln 2: the head loses more to miscalibration than its AUC earns.
What epoch selection sees. The served epoch is chosen on the combined validation loss. Its epoch-to-epoch swings (bars) against the entire achievable direction gain (red line, three horizons, weighted): selection is blind to direction.
Who pulls the price head, and where. Three proper terms agree on the conditional mean E[Y|x]. Three priors pull elsewhere: the trend term toward the last move, coherence toward ordered magnitudes, and the volatility term toward predictions as widely spread as the targets. With R² ≈ 0, the conditional mean's spread is √R² × std(y), almost zero. The vol term asks for std(y), about 1.1. Move R².
05

Learning an indicator

Every indicator is built from exponential moving averages. A period p enters as α = 2/(p+1) through a logit ℓ, so ℓ behaves like −log(p−1): one gradient step changes the period by a fixed fraction, whatever its size. Inside a 60-bar window the kernel is cut at the first bar, which takes all the older weight. Move the period.

$$e_t=(1-\alpha)^t x_0+\sum_{k=1}^{t}\alpha(1-\alpha)^{t-k}x_k,\qquad \alpha_{b,i}=\sigma\!\big(\ell_i+0.5\tanh(Wz_b+c)_i\big),\qquad \frac{\partial e_t}{\partial \ell}=(1-\alpha)\sum_{j\ge0}(1-\alpha)^j\,\Delta e_{t-j}$$
How strongly the loss can feel the period: mean |∂e59/∂ℓ| on simulated random-walk windows, against p. The signal vanishes as p → 1 (the average becomes the price) and fades beyond the window (only α × weight is identified). The shaded band is the allowed range [2, 60]; the per-window shift lets the applied period leave it (dev blocks: 1.6 to 74.7).

The search it replaces

A manual search enumerates settings. The network does two things at once: it moves 18 periods continuously, and it learns the combination rule as a function with 296,591 parameters. Here are the numbers, and what the six runs say it actually found.

Size of the grid (log₁₀ of the number of combinations) and the GPU time a full search would take at 22.6 minutes per training.
Cost of each way to search (GPU-hours, log scale). Gradient descent moves all periods jointly in one training. The catch: it minimises the training loss, not the dev-fold Sharpe, and it stays near where it starts.

Periods over training, all six runs. The fast legs keep falling to the floor of 2 until early stopping cuts them. The indicator learning rate never decays (it stays 0.005 while the main rate halves), so the periods drift rather than settle.
07

Architecture

The 18 periods are 0.006% of the parameters. 44.6% sit in one temporal attention. A block labelled "attend across indicators" actually attends across the 128 GRU units, with the 60 time positions as features. That block and the final Flatten → Dense tie the model to the 60-bar window.

Where the 296,591 parameters are (model built on CPU from the leader's configuration).
Memory against window length. Two tensor groups grow as L²: the batched EWMA weights [B, 18, L, L] and the attention scores [B, 8, L, L]. One float32 copy each; the backward pass keeps several. The dashed line is the GPU's 12 GB. This is an estimate from the shapes, not a profile.
08

How general it is

Ticker, bar size, window and horizons are meant to be configuration (D-022). Today most of them are still measured in bars of the reference setup, and the inputs are scaled in dollars, so one number carries both the price level and the volatility.

The same volatility in two units. The standard deviation of 10-minute moves in two months a year apart. In basis points they differ by 12%; in the dollar units the model reads, by 40%. The model has to learn the price level too.
