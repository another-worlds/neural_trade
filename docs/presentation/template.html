Chapter 2 · The climb

Twenty-four hypotheses, one change that mattered

We started from a 7-day model that lost money at 26 bps per round trip, and tried to raise both the model's predictive power and the strategy's P&L. Most lines of attack were negative. The two changes that moved the result were longer training and zero trading costs.

Return per ~32-day evaluation block, by milestone. Bars below −25% are cut and labelled with their value. Click a bar or a card to see what that step taught us.
The direction ceiling. Best direction AUC from every source we tried: the network, a logistic baseline, order flow, futures, conditional buckets. A stable 60% hit rate needs about 0.64. Everything sits between 0.52 and 0.56.
Maths and stability screen: 960 trials on 6-hour training blocks. They found no broken maths, but no configuration that recovers direction either. Almost every failure is a run whose gradient hit the clip on more than half its steps; extreme loss weights cause most of them.

Chapter 6 · Backtest

1,968 small bets on a falling market

Price, trades, equity and drawdown, on one time axis. Drag to zoom (double-click resets). The line is every 1-minute close, drawn at the end of its bar; a marker is the fill at the next bar's open (the decision is made on the close just before it). ▲ long, ▼ short, filled = winning trade.
Against luck. 100 random strategies with the same trade frequency and holding time (grey, 5th–95th percentile shaded) against C1 (gold).
Trade returns in basis points. Wins and losses are almost symmetric around zero; the mean is +0.8 bps. The edge is a slight shift of the whole distribution, not a few big winners.
By confidence at entry (distance of P(up) from its median), in quintiles. Confidence does not rank the trades; the most confident fifth earns least.
By volatility at entry (predicted σ, terciles). The edge lives in calm minutes: the calmest third wins 62%, while the most volatile third loses.
How trades end: reversal through the median line, the 15-bar time limit, or the 2σ stop.
Daily P&L (UTC days). The gains come from many ordinary days, not from one lucky day.
By hour of day (UTC, entry time): total P&L in $ and trades per hour.

Chapter 7 · Why it went this way

A 0.8 bps edge, compounded 1,968 times

The whole result is arithmetic: a tiny, stable average edge per trade multiplied by many trades. Costs decide everything. The same model and trades turn +16.7% into −99% at the 26 bps the project paid before D-044, and into a loss at about 1 bps.

Try it. Compounded return = (1 + (edge − cost) / 10⁴)trades − 1. The defaults are C1's measured numbers.
